# Asset direction and production

Use for hero imagery and other substantial website assets, including stock,
generated media and interactive scenes. Resolve the idea with the designer
before commissioning production. Reuse an already agreed direction and budget;
the checkpoint is for unresolved decisions, not repeated permission requests.

## 1. Give each asset a job

Read the offer, audience, copy and available client material. Write one sentence:
“This asset helps [visitor] understand or believe [specific benefit/process/proof]
so they can [next action].” Name what the visitor should recognize without an
explanation. If its only job is atmosphere, say so and explain why that atmosphere
helps this offer.

Check relevance before visual polish: does the scene communicate this service,
product or outcome, or could it decorate any business? Keep factual proof tied
to authentic material. Generated technicians, premises, work and outcomes are
illustrations, never evidence of the client's actual work.

## 2. Brainstorm with the designer

When the idea is unresolved, present two or three distinct concepts, not prompt
variations. For each, give:

- The literal scene and the customer meaning; why it belongs with this headline.
- A reference, existing image or simple layout sketch showing subject and copy.
- Asset route: client media, licensed stock, illustration/generated still, CSS/SVG,
  video or Three.js; include a cheaper route where it can serve the same job.
- Main tradeoff, likely production cost and whether motion adds useful information.

Recommend one and ask a focused question the designer can react to. Incorporate
their response and show the revised idea when the meaning changes. Use existing
assets, references and uncharged sketches for this discussion. Paid concept
previews count against the budget and need authorization too.

An approved overall site look does not settle an unresolved asset concept. If
the user explicitly delegates asset direction, record the recommended choice
and rationale and proceed within their existing spending authorization. Otherwise
wait for the concept choice while continuing copy, layout and other independent work.

For example, on a pest-control site a beautiful house cutaway only earns its place
if it explains a real service, such as entry-point inspection and exclusion. It
should show recognizable entry points and the relevant intervention, supported
by verified service information. Other concepts might be authentic technician
inspection photography for trust, or a calm home exterior with a restrained
illustrated protection perimeter for reassurance. Choose from the client's actual
offer and desired tone; none is a universal pest-control hero. Avoid a frightening
infestation image when the intended message is calm, discreet help.

**Done when:** the chosen idea has a clear visitor meaning, composition and route,
and the designer's choice or explicit delegation is recorded.

## 3. Scope the spend before production

Check current pricing through the selected provider's tools or documentation.
Compare a small shortlist of suitable routes/models for this asset, including
an existing, stock or still-based option. Choose the least expensive route that
can meet the brief, with an explicit reason for any premium model. Availability,
provider defaults and reputation do not establish suitability.

Present a concrete production plan: provider/model, output count, dimensions,
duration and audio where applicable, estimated cost per output, total batch cost,
retry allowance and maximum spend. Include already incurred project media spend.
If pricing is uncertain, identify the uncertainty and obtain a usable cap before
running paid work. A request to build a site is not an unlimited media budget.
Reuse an existing explicit cap when it covers this work; otherwise ask for one
after the concept and plan are concrete. Never infer approval from silence.

Start with one candidate or a deliberately small authorized exploration batch.
Record charges, including failed/rejected attempts when charged, and remaining
budget. Stop at the cap or attempt limit. If a batch misses the concept, diagnose
the mismatch and revise the plan instead of buying more variants automatically.

## 4. Prove the still in the layout

Use the approved concept to source or make a draft still. Place it with the real
headline and action at desktop and phone sizes before producing a full asset set.
Inspect subject accuracy, service relevance, crop, copy space, contrast and factual
implications. Show the in-layout preview for feedback while changes are cheap.
If asset review was delegated, record the same inspection and continue.

Accept on both meaning and execution. A pretty but irrelevant asset returns to
concept selection; a relevant asset with a crop problem needs composition/editing.
Expand a consistent asset family only after the representative asset passes.

## 5. Decide whether motion earns another production step

Describe exactly what changes over time and how that helps the visitor. A still,
small CSS/SVG animation or existing footage may already do the job. Use generated
video when its specific movement is worth the production and page-loading cost.
Three.js needs the same justification for depth or interaction.

Before paid video, show the approved still in context, the proposed movement,
duration, provider/model comparison, expected charge and static fallback. Proceed
only when concept, motion and spend are covered by the user's authorization.
An image budget or approved image does not automatically authorize video.
Choose audio only when the experience needs it; a background loop normally needs
no generated audio. Do not silently upgrade the model, resolution or duration.

Inspect the entire result and its poster in the actual hero. Check focal stability,
artifacts, loop seam, readability, mobile framing and reduced-motion fallback.
Reject video that adds expense or distraction without improving the approved idea.

## Decision record

Keep this in the project's existing design document rather than a separate log:

| Asset | Visitor job and chosen concept | Approval/delegation | Route/model and rationale | Batch/cap | Actual spend/remaining | Preview and decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Link sources/licenses, prompts/settings, accepted media and rejected attempts.
Distinguish estimated from actual charges. Report unavailable cost data explicitly.
Hand off the selected assets, mobile crops, posters/fallbacks, remaining decisions
and total known spend alongside the working page.
