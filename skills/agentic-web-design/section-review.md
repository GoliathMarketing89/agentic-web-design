# Section boards and the full-page storyboard

Use before implementing a new page or materially redesigning a section. The agent does the reference legwork and prepares concrete recommendations; the designer spends time choosing and refining. Scale to the requested scope and reuse decisions already made.

## 1. One complete UX/copy outline

Read project research and drafts first. Give sections stable IDs such as S01 Hero, S02 Services and S03 Process. Present the complete reading order, including navigation and footer. For each section, list the visitor question, purpose, headline, body text, proof/content slots, intended image role, CTA and actual destination or behavior. Mark missing evidence and provisional copy.

Agree this outline as a whole before selecting layouts. An approved business goal or hero is not approval of every lower section. Avoid imposing the same marketing arc on sites with different visitor tasks.

## 2. A visible board for every substantial section

Search the shared real-example bank, linked gallery, screenshots and rebuild notes before mining more. Shortlist two or three distinct candidates per section. A compact footer or repeated utility component can share a board; substantive sections below the hero still need visible decisions. An already-selected reference can be carried forward with its approval recorded.

For each candidate show:

- A stable option ID, source name/URL and an actual screenshot of the relevant section at a readable scale.
- Mobile evidence when available, plus the intended adaptation if the source behavior needs to change. Identify missing evidence; a resized desktop screenshot is not a mobile capture.
- The specific structural idea to borrow: hierarchy, asymmetric grouping, image/copy proportion, content sequence or interaction. State how it serves this section's visitor question and fits the amount of copy.
- What changes for this brand, which assets it needs, and an important tradeoff.

Place the proposed section copy beside the options and recommend one with a brief reason. Examples should differ in composition or reading model, not just colors. Fit a bento to the relationships in the material; do not make boxes simply to add visual variety.

Render the visuals directly in chat or a reviewable local board. A Markdown file that only contains source links is not a visual board. Use actual reference captures for examples; label schematic studies or reconstructed adaptations clearly. Reuse suitable saved captures and record capture dates/source limitations. Refresh unavailable, stale or ambiguous references when they affect the decision. Avoid new paid assets just to prepare a selection board; asset production follows the existing budget workflow.

**Completion:** the designer has been shown all section choices and recommendations in a form they can inspect without opening every source site.

## 3. One short choice round, then focused refinement

Invite a compact response such as “S02 A, S03 B, S06 more editorial.” The designer can accept all recommended options together. Revise only unresolved choices, using a rough adaptation with the real copy when a reference alone does not explain the proposal.

Keep an approval record in the project's existing design document:

| Section ID | Purpose and copy | Options shown | Selected structure and adaptation | Asset status | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| S02 Services | Find the relevant service; copy recorded above | A / B | Chosen grouping and why it fits | Existing / provisional / needs production | Agreed / unresolved / explicitly delegated |

Record what the feedback covered. Preserve earlier approvals; reopen a choice when its meaning changes, not for routine execution fixes. Explicit delegation permits the agent to choose and record a recommendation; it does not supply a media budget or eliminate the board and storyboard deliverables.

## 4. Agree the full-page storyboard

Arrange the selected section compositions into one scrollable visual or contact sheet before production implementation. Use real copy, recognizable asset placeholders and approximate proportions. Show enough desktop composition and mobile order to assess the combination. A reference collage is useful evidence, but identify the proposed composition separately so it is not mistaken for the finished site.

Review:

- Coverage: every agreed UX job, content requirement and destination is represented.
- Rhythm: image scale, density, spacing, background changes and transitions create an intentional sequence.
- Repetition: repeated grids, splits, cards and imagery are deliberate; several attractive sections can still make a generic page.
- Coherence: selected examples have been translated into one brand rather than stitched together as unrelated styles.
- Mobile: hierarchy, source order, crop and primary action remain understandable.
- Feasibility: required assets, interactions and platform capabilities are plausible within scope and budget.

**Completion:** the designer has seen and agreed the combined storyboard, or explicitly delegated that decision. General aesthetic approval alone does not satisfy this criterion. Keep the unresolved stage visible and continue independent work while waiting; silence is not a decision.

## 5. Progress reviews and critique

Implement a representative slice, then small groups of sections. Show actual rendered desktop/mobile views beside the agreed references, with a brief account of what changed and which decision, if any, is needed. Do not stop for permission on ordinary typography, spacing or responsive fixes within the selected direction.

A material change to composition, imagery meaning, copy purpose or interaction gets a concrete revised proposal before it is repeated across the site. Reuse a proven shared section on other pages when the agreement covers that reuse.

Give the critic the full UX, selected reference screenshots, storyboard and rendered evidence for both major sections and the full page. Review section-specific design quality and overall distinctiveness, not just visual cleanliness. Keep scores separate from concrete findings and designer preference. Approval to build, a draft visual score, production verification and publication authorization are different decisions.
