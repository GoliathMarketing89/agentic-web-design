# Goal-driven pages and sections

Use before writing page outlines, selecting section references or commissioning assets. Keep the decisions in the existing design record and storyboard. The agent prepares the map from available research and makes a recommendation; resolve only consequential gaps in the existing three touchpoints.

## Site goal → page role → section contribution

Define the site's primary outcome in visitor and business terms: a qualified enquiry, purchase, booking, registration or a particular learning outcome. Include the intended audience, offer and what successful completion looks like. “Submit a form” needs the form's purpose and what follows; a click is not a completed enquiry or purchase.

Assign each required page a role in that journey. Record likely arrival intent and awareness, the decision or task it must advance, main evidence needed, primary next action and its actual destination/behavior. Support direct entry from search or campaigns; do not assume visitors have read the homepage. Keep one clear primary outcome per page, with useful subordinate routes when appropriate.

| Page role | Example purpose on a home-service site |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | Establish the relevant offer and reasons to choose the provider; route visitors to service or enquiry |
| Service/pest page | Help someone seeking that service evaluate this provider's solution and take the next step |
| About/team | Establish relevant trust, expertise and service approach; make the service/enquiry route easy to find |
| Location | Establish actual service availability and local relevance; route to a suitable enquiry |
| Educational article | Answer the reader's information need well; offer a relevant service route where useful |
| Enquiry/booking | Make the requested action clear and manageable; show validation, confirmation and truthful next-step expectations |

These are examples, not a mandatory sitemap. Ecommerce, applications, public information and other sites have different outcomes. Utility, policy, support and educational pages can serve their own tasks without being turned into sales letters. Record how they belong in the site rather than forcing a lead CTA into every section.

For each substantial section, record one row in the existing outline:

| Section | Visitor question/objection | Intended change in understanding, confidence or action | Supported answer/evidence | Visual treatment and asset | Next action or contribution to page goal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

A section can orient, explain value, demonstrate a solution, establish trust, reduce friction or enable action. It need not have its own button. Topic relevance, word count and filling a preferred layout are insufficient reasons to include it. Remove, combine, shorten or link out sections that do not contribute.

## Make the commercial argument specific

For a service page, cover the provider's relevant solution, what the customer can expect, supported reasons to choose it and objections that affect the enquiry. Use the visitor's readiness to decide how much problem education is useful. Keep diagnosis/identification material brief or link to a separate article when it distracts from choosing the provider. Do not assume all traffic has the same knowledge.

For example, a termite-service page may need treatment approach, recurrence prevention, evidence and visit expectations if those belong to the verified offer. A long taxonomy of termites cannot replace that argument. A separate identification article may legitimately devote most of its space to identification.

## Design the route to completion

Connect actions across pages: discovery → relevant offer/evidence → enquiry or purchase → confirmation/next steps. Keep ready-to-act visitors able to proceed early; others can inspect evidence first. Navigation, CTA labels, forms and destination pages must agree about what happens. Inspect for circular routes, dead ends, competing primary actions and unnecessary detours.

Exercise the main journey from representative entry pages on desktop and phone. Confirm the action's result at the implemented scope; label mocks or disconnected services in handoff notes. Distinguish observed behavior from measured business results. Propose useful success events when measurement is in scope; do not install analytics or claim conversion improvements from an attractive preview alone.

## Review purpose and visual execution together

Check every page and substantial section against its recorded role, using the actual copy, composition, imagery and interactions. A beautiful section that sends visitors toward the wrong task needs revision. A correct argument presented as repetitive text blocks also needs revision against the agreed visual direction; follow the visual plan in [creative-review.md](creative-review.md).

Keep this map lightweight: extend the existing UX/section table and final review, rather than creating a separate workshop or requiring the designer to justify each row. Reuse explicit approvals and delegated decisions.
