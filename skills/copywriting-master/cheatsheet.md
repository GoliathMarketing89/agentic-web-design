# Cheatsheet

## Before writing: The Three Questions (Breakthrough)
| Question | Tells you |
|---|---|
| 1. What is the mass desire? | The force that creates the market |
| 2. How much do they know about your product? (Awareness) | Where the headline starts |
| 3. How many products before yours? (Sophistication) | The strategy of the headline |

## The Five Stages of Sophistication (Breakthrough)
| Stage | Situation | Strategy | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | You are first | Be simple, direct, not fancy | "NOW! LOSE UGLY FAT!" |
| 2 | Direct claim still works | Enlarge to the limit | "LOSE UP TO 47 POUNDS IN 4 WEEKS" |
| 3 | Heard all claims | New mechanism in headline | "FLOATS FAT RIGHT OUT OF YOUR BODY!" |
| 4 | Mechanism being copied | Elaborate the mechanism | "FIRST NO-DIET REDUCING WONDER DRUG!" |
| 5 | Field exhausted | Identification | "WHY MEN CRACK..." |

## The Five States of Awareness (Breakthrough)
| State | Prospect knows | Headline does |
|---|---|---|
| Most Aware | Product, what it does, wants it | Name product + bargain price |
| Knows of Product | Product, not fully convinced | Display name + superiority |
| New Products | Wants what it does, not that it exists | Name desire/solution + prove + mechanism |
| Solves Needs | Has a need, not the connection | Name need/solution + dramatize + product |
| Completely Unaware | Not aware of desire/need | Identification headline |

## Decision Rules
- **Sophistication ladder**: first → be simple; second → enlarge; heard it all → mechanize; mechanism copied → elaborate; field dead → identify. (Breakthrough)
- **Desire**: exploit existing desire for the Amplification Effect ($1 → $50–$100); never try to create desire — that's education and you lose money. (Breakthrough)
- **Claim too powerful to believe raw** → build a believability-bridge before stating it. (Breakthrough)
- **Product too hard** → simplify it; **too unimportant** → escalate it; **too expensive** → switch the comparison. (Breakthrough)
- **Prospect asks "How does it work?"** → name, describe, or feature the mechanism. (Breakthrough)
- **Attacking a competitor** → always provide the solution to the weakness you attack, or you evoke skepticism. (Breakthrough)
- **Unaware market** → the prospect must identify with the headline before he can buy from it. (Breakthrough)
- **Market seems dead** → it's usually just sophisticated; the desire never fades, only the method of tapping it. (Breakthrough)
- **Combining emotional images** → 1 + 1 can equal 10; juxtapose claims to reinforce. (Breakthrough)
- **Sell the benefit of the benefit** → keep asking "what's the benefit of that?" until you reach the end result. (Cashvertising/Vosler)
- **Appeal choice** → use a Life-Force 8 desire when one fits; secondary wants only as support. (Cashvertising)
- **Persuasion depth** → high-involvement buys need central-route logic; low-involvement runs on peripheral cues (CLARCCS). (Cashvertising)
- **Readability** → aim Flesch ~72+, ~11-word sentences, 70-80% one-syllable words. (Cashvertising)
- **Headline** → put the biggest benefit in it; 60% read only headlines. (Cashvertising)
- **Offer without a deadline** → reads as always available; add hard or soft scarcity. (Cashvertising)
- **Long copy** → well-written long copy outsells short; "copy can never be too long, only too boring." (Cashvertising)
- **Guarantee** → offer the longest, strongest in your industry; boosts sales AND cuts returns. (Cashvertising)
- **Price barrier** → amortize ("just $1.25 a day"), diminish, or boost perceived value. (Cashvertising)
- **Installments** → for products over $15, "3 easy payments" boosts response ~15%. (Cashvertising)
- **Coupon** → say "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" not "50% Off"; free is more powerful. (Cashvertising)
- **Skeptical reader** → use the "Even if…" intro to crush the jugular objection. (Vosler)
- **Reader new to topic** → frame simply with Blooms Taxonomy to build trust. (Vosler)
- **Need real pains/desires** → use Community Arbitrage for authentic voice. (Vosler)
- **Stuck / no inspiration** → use the Imitation Game; build on what works. (Vosler)
- **Second draft** → Kintsugi Revision (shorten, add metaphor, emotion); **final pass** → Vocal Editing (read aloud). (Vosler)
- **CTA placement** → delay it (Vosler: step 19 of 21); the reader must be ready first.
- **Ethical line** → persuasion over manipulation; manipulation relies on false promises. (Vosler)
- **Final gate** → run every ad through the 46-Point Killer Ad Checklist before publishing. (Cashvertising)
