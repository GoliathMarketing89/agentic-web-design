# Chapter 4: Hot Lists: 101 Easy Ways to Boost Your Ad Response

## Core Idea
101 easy, actionable ways to boost ad response, organized into five lists: 22 Response Superchargers, 9 Ways to Convey Value, 13 Ways to Make Buying Easy, 11 Ways to Boost Coupon Returns, and a 46-Point "Killer Ad" Checklist.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **22 Response Superchargers**: (1) forget style, sell instead; (2) scream "Free Information!"; (3) write short sentences; (4) use short, simple words; (5) write long copy; (6) boil it down, cut the fluff; (7) stir desire by piling on benefits; (8) show what you're selling — action shots best; (9) get personal — say you, you, you; (10) use selling subheads to break up long copy; (11) put selling captions under photos; (12) write powerful visual adjectives; (13) sell your product, not your competitor's; (14) don't hold back, give the full sell now; (15) always include testimonials; (16) make it ridiculously easy to act; (17) include a response coupon; (18) set a deadline to break inertia; (19) offer a free gift for quick replies; (20) say "Order Now!"; (21) offer free shipping; (22) boost response 50%+ with a "Bill Me" or credit option.
  - When to use: as a pre-flight checklist for any ad.
- **9 Ways to Convey Value**: (1) scream "Sale!"; (2) give them a coupon; (3) diminish the price ("less than a cup of coffee a day"); (4) explain why the price is low ("our boss ordered too many!"); (5) amortize it ("just $1.25 a day"); (6) boost the value — tell what it's worth, not only what it costs; (7) tell how much others have paid; (8) create scarcity with deadlines; (9) employ psychological pricing.
  - When to use: to make price feel like a bargain.
- **13 Ways to Make Buying Easy**: (1) give street, email, and web address; (2) give phone number; (3) provide directions and parking advice; (4) say "It's Easy to Order..."; (5) accept phone orders; (6) mail orders; (7) online orders; (8) fax orders; (9) credit cards; (10) personal checks; (11) get a toll-free number; (12) include a long, strong guarantee — longer than your competition's; (13) offer installment payments for products over $15 ("3 easy payments of just $10.99"), shown to boost response 15%.
  - When to use: to remove every obstacle between desire and purchase.
- **11 Ways to Boost Coupon Returns**: (1) tell them in the headline/subhead to return the coupon; (2) say "Buy 1 Get 1 Free!" instead of "50% Off"; (3) use a big "FREE!" at the top; (4) tell what the coupon brings, and say it again inside the coupon; (5) show it with a small photo; (6) use a bold coupon border; (7) set a hard or soft deadline; (8) provide check-off boxes; (9) say "Valuable Coupon" at the top; (10) give room for fill-ins; (11) point to the coupon with bold arrows.
  - When to use: any coupon or response device.
- **46-Point "Killer Ad" Checklist**: A self-audit across four sections — Headline (9 checks), Body Copy: First Sentence (5), Body Copy: General (20), Layout and Design (12). Key checks: headline features the biggest benefit; headline in initial caps (ALL CAPS only for ~4-5 words); headline in quotes (boosts reading 25%); body progresses logically (get attention → stimulate interest → build desire → offer proof → ask for action); sell one product at a time; use selling subheads; use extreme specificity; serif type for print, sans-serif for web; ask for the sale outright; set a deadline; use testimonials; business name/phone large; key the ad to track response; professional design; break the headline at the right words; sufficient white space; indent paragraphs; minimum elements; relevant art; minimum typestyles (1-2, max 3); feature a person looking at you.
  - When to use: as the final quality gate before publishing any ad.

## Key Concepts
- **Response supercharger**: A single tactic that lifts response (benefits, testimonials, deadlines, ease).
- **Value conveyance**: Techniques that make price feel small or the value feel large.
- **Psychological pricing**: Odd-end prices (77, 95, 99) suggest value; rounded prices suggest prestige.
- **Amortization**: Breaking price into small units ("just $1.25 a day") to diminish perceived cost.
- **Killer Ad Checklist**: The 46-point self-audit for ad quality.
- **Keying**: Adding a code to an ad to track which response it generated.

## Mental Models
- Use the 46-Point Checklist as your final gate — the more boxes you check, the better the ad.
- Use "Buy 1 Get 1 Free" instead of "50% Off" when you want more coupon redemptions — free is a more powerful word.
- Use amortization and diminishing when price is a barrier — "less than a cup of coffee a day."
- Use installment payments when the product is over $15 — it boosts response ~15%.
- Use a deadline whenever the offer can carry one — it breaks inertia.

## Anti-patterns
- **No deadline**: an offer without urgency reads as always available.
- **Selling features instead of benefits**: doesn't answer the WIIFM.
- **Short copy**: fails to convince the reader who needs details.
- **Amateur design and too many elements**: clutters the ad and kills focus.
- **No testimonials**: you're missing the most persuasive proof available.
- **Not asking for the sale**: an ad that informs but doesn't ask for action is a lousy investment.

## Worked Example
The 46-Point Checklist applied to a headline: a strong headline (1) features the product's biggest benefit, (2) elicits an emotional response, (3) uses a headline starter from Chapter 3, (4) is significantly larger and boldfaced, (5) makes an offer, (6) is authoritative, (7) is set in initial caps (ALL CAPS only for ~4-5 words), and (8) is in quotes to boost reading 25%. The body then progresses through the five-step arc — attention, interest, desire, proof, action — and the ad closes by asking for the sale outright with a deadline.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run every ad through the 46-Point Killer Ad Checklist before publishing.
2. Pile on benefits, testimonials, and a strong guarantee — they answer the WIIFM and quell fear.
3. Make buying ridiculously easy: multiple payment/order options, directions, toll-free number, installments.
4. Convey value by diminishing, amortizing, and boosting perceived worth.
5. Use deadlines, "Buy 1 Get 1 Free," and bold coupon borders to lift coupon returns.
6. Always ask for the sale and set a deadline.
