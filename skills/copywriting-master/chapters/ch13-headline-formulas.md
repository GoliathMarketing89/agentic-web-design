# Chapter 13: Headline Formulas

## Core Idea
Headlines are the highest-leverage words you write — "five times as many people read the headline as read the body copy" (Ogilvy). The swipe files of hundreds of copywriters yield ~39+ swipeable headline formulas, plus the 33 Headline Prompts and the 41 Classic Headline Templates. Every subhead and crosshead deserves the same attention as the primary headline.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Headline Starter Kit** (basic formulas):
  - For ___ who want to ___
  - Get ___ without the ___
  - Helping ____ do ____
  - The only ____ that doesn't ____
  - The (good news) inside (bad news)
- **Attention-Grabbing Headline = "Audience Identifier" + "Hook" + "Benefit"**.
- **Headline Benefit Modifiers** (append to any hook):
  - "... and How XYZ Saved More Time & Money By Making This Small Change."
  - "... and Why Now More Than Ever XYZ is Becoming More Common Place."
  - "... and What You Can Do To Change XYZ So You Can Enjoy More Freedom."
  - "... and What They Did to Increase Profits At The Same Time."
  - "... and Why You're Missing Out on XYZ if You're Not Embracing The Change."
- **The Imitation Game headline structure**: "The [noun] Who Perfected The [topic]" → expand with a modifier = End Result + Time Frame, Fear of Loss, Fear of Change, or Fear of Unknown + Hope.

## Key Concepts
- **A headline must accomplish at least one of**: capture attention, inspire thought, confirm suspicion, create intrigue, promise powerfully, question reality.
- **The 33 Headline Prompts**: Ask a question; Reference current events; Create your own terms; Reveal news (New/Introducing); Tell the reader to do something; Give stats; Make a comparison; Promise useful information; Direct offer; Tell a (quick) story; Make a recommendation; State benefit; Use a testimonial; Arouse curiosity; Promise to reveal a secret; Be ultra specific; Target a section of your audience; Time-based headline; Stress urgency/scarcity of savings/value; Deliver good news; Challenge the reader; Highlight your guarantee; State the price (as benefit); Set up a (seemingly) contradiction; Address reader objection/concern; "As Crazy As it Sounds:"; Take them to the promise land; Demonstrate ROI; Reason-why headline; List/answer questions; Stress cost-saving value; Highlight cost of mistakes; State/deliver on reader's goals.

## Mental Models
- Use the swipe formulas when you need a headline fast; consult a swipe file every time you write a headline.
- Think of every subhead and crosshead as a headline needing the same attention.
- Use the Imitation Game to adapt a trending headline's structure to your niche.

## Anti-patterns
- **Boring the reader**: "we're in the business of entertainment; or at the very least it's our job not to bore our reader."
- **Neglecting subheads**: every subhead and crosshead needs the same attention as the primary headline.
- **Copying instead of imitating**: imitation builds on what's working; copying is plagiarism.

## Worked Example
The Imitation Game: trending headline "The Man Who Perfected The Laugh Track" → structure "The [noun] Who Perfected The [topic]" → adapted for real-estate agents: "Meet The [Real Estate Agent] Who Perfected The [Short Sale], and Learn How it 4x'd His Agency's Profits in 6 Months" (modifier = End Result + Time Frame).

## Key Takeaways
1. Spend the majority of your time on the headline — it's 80 cents of your dollar.
2. Use the 33 Headline Prompts as a checklist when stuck.
3. Append a benefit modifier to any hook to add "what's in it for them."
4. Imitate the structure of trending headlines, then adapt to your niche.
5. Give every subhead and crosshead the same attention as the primary headline.

## Connects To
- **Ch 10**: headlines feed the Attention step of the 7-A arc.
- **Ch 12**: every sales-letter formula opens with a headline.
- **Ch 11**: the "Call to Adventure" is often delivered through the headline.

### Brian Clark's 10 Sure-Fire Headlines
1. Who Else Wants [blank]? — classic social proof strategy implying existing consensus desire.
2. The Secret of [blank] — share insider knowledge translated into a reader benefit.
3. Here is a Method That is Helping [blank] to [blank] — identify target audience and the benefit you provide.
4. Little Known Ways to [blank] — more intriguing version of "The Secret of…".
5. Get Rid of [problem] Once and For All — identifies a painful problem or unfulfilled desire.
6. Here's a Quick Way to [solve a problem] — people love quick and easy.
7. Now You Can Have [something desirable] [great circumstance] — the "have your cake and eat it too" headline.
8. [Do something] like [world-class example] — "Be Like Mike" (Gatorade/Michael Jordan).
9. Have a [or] Build a [blank] You Can Be Proud Of.
10. [blank] — appeal to vanity, dissatisfaction, or shame.

### 41 Classic Copywriting Headline Templates (Harrison Amy)
1. Say something that gets attention.
2. Tell them why they should be interested. (Expand on CSI)
3. Tell them why they should believe what you are saying is true.
4. Prove it is true.
5. Itemize and describe all benefits.
6. Tell them how to order.
7. Tell them to order now.

### Jason Brewer's 3 Sure-Fire Website Copy Templates
- **Copy Template #1: Inspire First** — Inspire and connect → Cut to the chase → Who should care.
- **Copy Template #2: Why, How, What** — Why (the story) → How (the method) → What (the offer).
- **Copy Template #3: Big shift in the world** — Big shift in the world → Winner and losers → Tease the promised land → Be the solution.
