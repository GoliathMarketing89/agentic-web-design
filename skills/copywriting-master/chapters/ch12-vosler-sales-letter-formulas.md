# Chapter 12: Sales Letter Formulas

## Core Idea
Direct-response sales letters are built from proven, repeatable formulas. Vosler compiles the tried-and-true formulas of the last 150 years — from AIDA to the Craig Clemens formula to Perry Belcher's 21-step formula — and stresses one counterintuitive rule: the call to action belongs late, not early.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **AIDA / AIDCA / IDCA**: Attention (Awareness) → Interest (Inspire) → Desire → Action. AIDCA adds Conviction (turn doubt into trust via testimonials, endorsements, statistics, research, demos, guarantees). IDCA skips Attention when the reader already has it (e.g., a landing page building on an ad).
- **Craig Clemens Sales Letter Formula**: Headline → Subhead → Questions that show you understand and push buttons → Powerful analogy or story → "It's not your fault and there's hope" → Give away content → Proof → What is it I'm offering? What's in it? → Who needs it? Who doesn't? → What makes it different? → Wrap up → Price → A guarantee → A close → A signature → Your P.S. → Your testimonials.
- **Perry Belcher's 21-Part Sales Letter Formula** (the 21-step formula):
  1. Call out to your audience (say whom it's best for)
  2. Get their attention with a big promise headline
  3. Backup the big promise with a quick explanation
  4. Identify the core or most painful problem
  5. Provide the solution to said problem
  6. Show pain and cost of development of solution
  7. Explain ease-of-use
  8. Show speed to achieve results
  9. Future pace (help the prospect visualize their improved future)
  10. Show your credentials
  11. Detail the benefits of the solution
  12. Insert social proof
  13. Make your offer (an irresistible one)
  14. Add your bonuses
  15. Build up the value of the copywriting solution + bonuses
  16. Finally reveal your price
  17. Inject scarcity (if any)
  18. Reduce risk with a guarantee
  19. **Call to action**
  20. Give a warning
  21. Close with a reminder
- **12-Step Foolproof Sales Letter Template** (David Frey): Get attention → Identify the problem → Provide the solution → Present your credentials → Show the benefits → Give social proof → Make your offer → Inject scarcity → Give a guarantee → Call to action → Give a warning → Close with a reminder.
- **Bob Stone's Sales Page Formula**: Step 1 Make a promise based on your most important benefit → Step 2 Immediately expand upon that benefit → Step 3 Tell the reader exactly what they'll get (tangibles + intangibles) → Step 4 Support with proof → Step 5 Tell the reader what they'll lose if they fail to act → Step 6 Rephrase prominent benefits → Step 7 Incite immediate action.
- **Star Story Solution**: Introduce the star of the story → Tell the star's story → Present the solution that helped the star achieve big things. Character-focused, short; great for lead-gen pages and personality brands.
- **The Nine-Point Copywriting Formula** (Frank Egner): Grab attention with a headline → Follow with an inspiring lede → Clearly define the product → Give a success story/case study → Add testimonials and endorsements → List special features/value-adds → State your value proposition → Move the prospect to action with specifics and urgency → Finish with a postscript.
- **HELLYEAH** (Brian McLeod): Holler and get their attention → Empathize with their pains (usually by sharing yours) → Lambast the things that led to the problem → Legwork — prove you're not full of it → Yes, you have the solution → Educate them on why your solution is best → Action — give them a clear action to take → Handle lingering doubts or objections.
- **PASTOR** (Ray Edwards): Person, problem, pain → Amplify the consequences of not solving → Story, solution, system → Testimonials → Offer (spend 80% of the offer talking about transformation) → Response (call to action).
- **The Jim Edwards VSL Method**: Open with a shocking statement → State the problem and why it's a big deal → Agitate the problem → Push the agitation to a deep emotional level → Introduce the solution → Highlight your credibility → Prove what you're saying is true → List the biggest things they'll get (features and benefits) → Specific reasons not to procrastinate → Close by asking for the purchase and reinforcing benefits.

## Key Concepts
- **The CTA is step 19 of 21**: "You should not be in a rush to supply a CTA. Yes, people will click CTAs that appear early — but are they ready for it? Wait until they're ready. Just. Wait."
- **Tangibles vs. intangibles**: tangibles are the components of the product (e.g., 37 downloadable HD videos); intangibles are the changes to their life (e.g., the time-saving power of a proven system).
- **The benefit of the benefit**: sell the deeper outcome, not the solution. "They're not selling a hair product, they're selling a confidence product."
- **The jugular objection**: the single most common objection; if not addressed, it loses the most people. Go for the jugular first.
- **Future pacing**: help the prospect visualize their vastly improved future.

## Mental Models
- Use the 21-step formula when you're seriously uninspired and need a complete long-form sales page skeleton.
- Use Star Story Solution when the piece is character-driven and short (lead-gen pages, personality brands).
- Use IDCA when the reader already has attention (landing page building on an ad).
- Use Bob Stone's formula for lead-gen pages; it's missing the build-up between steps 2 and 3, so it's weaker for full sales pages.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rushing the CTA**: the biggest mistake; delay it until the prospect is ready.
- **Selling the solution, not the benefit of the benefit**: sell the confidence, not the hair product.
- **Ho-hum guarantees**: don't be casual about the critical risk-reducer.
- **Distracting links**: eliminate all distracting links from a sales page.

## Worked Example
The lead-generation funnel vendor: a prospect wants a traditional website (home, about, contact pages). The challenger vendor says "You don't need a website, you need an online lead generation funnel," teaches what it is, and sells the results (more leads, more revenue). This "eureka moment" mindset change almost always results in a closed deal. The copy asks: "Am I challenging my customer to think differently about their problem?"

## Key Takeaways
1. Delay the CTA until the prospect is ready — it's step 19 of 21.
2. Sell the benefit of the benefit, not the solution.
3. Challenge the prospect's perspective on their problem to create a eureka moment.
4. Use the "Even if…" intro to crush the jugular objection early.
5. Give away content and proof before the offer to build trust.
