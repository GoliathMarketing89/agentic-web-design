# Chapter 14: Tactical Execution, Editing & The Imitation Game

## Core Idea
Copywriting is a skill you can learn by imitation, not just talent. Vosler's tactical methods — the Imitation Game, Amazon R&D, Community Arbitrage, Cognitive Manipulation, and the Kintsugi/Vocal editing processes — turn writing from a guessing game into a repeatable system.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Imitation Game (Method 1)**: harness the structure of trending content to craft engaging copy quickly.
  - When to use: when starting out, or whenever you lack inspiration.
  - How: 6 steps — (1) Write a brief of what the reader should gain and do; (2) Research trending headlines on digg, reddit, news.google, medium, buzzfeed, cracked; (3) Formulate by rewriting trending headlines imitating their style with your subject; (4) Develop by analyzing why the headline works (curiosity, belief confirmation, open loop); (5) Adapt the structure to your topic; (6) Expand with a benefit modifier (End Result + Time Frame, Fear of Loss, Fear of Change, Fear of Unknown + Hope).
- **Amazon R&D (Method 2)**: use the forces behind best-selling books' subtitles to understand your audience.
  - When to use: to find what's enticing people in your niche.
  - How: (1) Write a brief; (2) Go to amazon.com's book section, select your niche, sort by best sellers; (3) Map the subtitle structure and expand it for your niche.
- **Community Arbitrage (Method 3)**: harness the power of community to create lists of benefits your leads actually care about.
  - When to use: to get into the head of your reader and find real pains/desires.
  - How: (1) Find a book closely related to your product on Amazon; (2) Read the "most helpful" reviews; (3) Highlight points that speak to the reader's inner dialog (yellow) and pull on emotions (red); (4) Extract the essence into headlines and bullet points.
- **Cognitive Manipulation (Method 4)**: ethically harness the built-in biases of your reader to increase sales.
  - When to use: to overcome bias-related objections and craft killer copy.
  - How: apply Anchoring, Bandwagon, Choice-supportive, Pro-innovation, and Zero-risk biases (see Key Concepts).
- **The Kintsugi Revision Process**: the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold — treat breakage and repair as part of the object's history, not something to disguise.
  - When to use: on the second draft.
  - How: revise to shorten sentence structure, say more with less, make language more interesting, remove redundant statements, add metaphors/similes, add flair and emotion.
- **The Vocal Editing Technique**: edit for clarity and readability by reading your copy aloud.
  - When to use: after the Kintsugi pass, to catch awkward phrasing.
  - How: read the piece out loud; where you stumble, revise.

## Key Concepts
- **The jugular objection**: the single most common objection; go for it first.
- **The "Even if…" intro**: start your intro with "Even if…" to go right for the jugular objection and disarm the skeptic.
- **Illusory superiority**: most people think they're better than average; their default response is "It might work for them, but not for me."
- **Bullet point formula**: (Feature) + (Benefit) + (Benefit of that benefit).
- **Fascinations**: bullets written to be so compelling and curiosity-piquing that desire is magnified.
- **Anchoring bias**: people over-rely on the first piece of information; set your own anchor comparing your price to something familiar.
- **Bandwagon effect**: adoption increases with the proportion who've already adopted; demonstrate the reader isn't alone.
- **Choice-supportive bias**: people retroactively ascribe positive attributes to a chosen option; reinforce the positive choice before and after the sale.
- **Pro-innovation bias**: the belief an innovation should be adopted by everyone; polish your product by explaining why it's innovative.
- **Zero-risk bias**: people prefer complete elimination of a risk even when alternatives reduce risk more; demonstrate value beyond simple money math.
- **Know Like Trust Challenge Convert (KLTCC)**: the sales sequence — get them to know you, like you, trust you, then challenge their perspective and convert.
- **Teach, Transform, Transact**: teaching earns trust; transform their understanding, then transact.
- **Blooms Taxonomy**: six levels of understanding (Knowledge → Comprehension → Application → Analysis → Synthesis → Evaluation) used to qualify your audience's experience and frame content complexity.
- **The benefit of the benefit**: sell the deeper outcome, not the solution.

## Mental Models
- Use the Imitation Game when you need a fast start; use Amazon R&D when you need to understand your niche's promise; use Community Arbitrage when you need real pains and desires.
- Think of research as smelting gold: dig ore → distill to useful metals → separate the gold → refine into bars → craft the ring.
- Use Blooms Taxonomy to determine how complex to frame your content based on your reader's level.
- Use the "tracing method": leverage tried-and-tested templates to blast through writer's block and avoid guesswork.

## Anti-patterns
- **Copying instead of imitating**: imitation builds on what's working; copying is plagiarism.
- **Skipping research**: if your audience knows more than you, you lose all leverage.
- **Rushing the CTA**: delay it until the prospect is ready.
- **Selling the solution, not the benefit of the benefit**: sell the confidence, not the hair product.
- **Unethical manipulation**: manipulation relies on false promises and distorted facts; persuasion relies on skillful demonstration of the facts.

## Worked Example
The Imitation Game applied to real estate: trending headline "The Man Who Perfected The Laugh Track" → structure "The [noun] Who Perfected The [topic]" → "Meet The [Real Estate Agent] Who Perfected The [Short Sale], and Learn How it 4x'd His Agency's Profits in 6 Months" (modifier = End Result + Time Frame).

## Key Takeaways
1. Imitation is not copying — it's building on what's already working.
2. Use the "Even if…" intro to crush the jugular objection early.
3. Write bullets as (Feature) + (Benefit) + (Benefit of that benefit).
4. Ethically harness cognitive biases (anchoring, bandwagon, choice-supportive, pro-innovation, zero-risk).
5. Edit with the Kintsugi process: shorten, remove redundancy, add metaphor and emotion.
6. Read your copy aloud (Vocal Editing) to catch awkward phrasing.
7. Use Blooms Taxonomy to frame content at your reader's level of understanding.
