# Glossary

**7-A Persuasion Arc** (Vosler) — long-form structure: Attention → Acknowledgement → Aggravate → Authority → Angst → Ambiguity → Appeal.
**Achievement Roles** (Breakthrough) — status/class/position roles (Executive, Wife, Man-On-His-Way-Up) the prospect wants to win; expressed by nouns as titles.
**AIDA** (Cashvertising) — Attention, Interest, Desire, Action; the classic sales-message structure.
**Amortization** (Cashvertising) — breaking price into small units ("just $1.25 a day") to diminish perceived cost.
**Amplification Effect** (Breakthrough) — $1 exploiting existing desire can create $50–$100 in sales; $1 creating desire (education) yields at best $1.
**Anchoring bias** (Vosler) — people over-rely on the first information; set your own anchor.
**Bandwagon effect** (Vosler/Cashvertising) — adoption rises with the proportion who've adopted; link product to aspirational/associative/dissociative groups.
**Belief** (Breakthrough) — the opinions, attitudes, prejudices the prospect lives by; a filter your copy must pass through.
**Belief Re-ranking** (Cashvertising) — change what people believe or its importance; easier to strengthen/weaken an existing belief than change it.
**Believability-Bridge** (Breakthrough) — an accepted image connecting what the prospect already believes to what you want him to believe.
**Benefit of the benefit** (Vosler) — sell the deeper outcome, not the solution.
**Camouflage** (Breakthrough) — technique 7: borrow conviction from believability stored in media, formats, phraseology, moods.
**The Catalog** (Breakthrough) — condensed listing of all the product's performances/benefits/applications; summarizes and hedges.
**Character Roles** (Breakthrough) — personality traits (virile, chic, brilliant) the prospect wants to express; expressed by adjectives.
**The Claim** (Breakthrough) — the promise of performance — what your product does to satisfy the dominant desire.
**CLARCCS** (Cashvertising) — Cialdini's six cues: Comparison, Liking, Authority, Reciprocation, Commitment/Consistency, Scarcity.
**Cognitive bias** (Vosler) — a mistake in reasoning/evaluating/remembering, often from holding preferences despite contrary information.
**Community Arbitrage** (Vosler) — research method using Amazon reviews to find real pains/desires.
**Concentration** (Breakthrough) — technique 6: destroy alternate ways to satisfy the desire by pointing out competitors' weaknesses and proving your product wins without them.
**The Creative Gamble** (Breakthrough) — risking the headline (not mentioning the product) to grasp attention and impel reading.
**Deadly Sincerity** (Breakthrough) — a mood that points out flaws in an offer so benefits are believed more deeply.
**Desires** (Breakthrough) — wants, needs, cravings that drive the prospect; you can expand, sharpen, channel, give them a goal.
**Diamond of Persuasion** (Vosler) — five strategies: Fan Their Flames, Pardon Their Errors, Defeat Their Demons, Prove Their Presumptions, Attack Their Enemies.
**Dominant Performance** (Breakthrough) — the one performance featured in the ad; the claim.
**Echo Ads** (Breakthrough) — ads that substitute words into a proven headline without respecting the unique product-market-timing relationship.
**Ego Morphing** (Cashvertising) — consumers buy to enhance egos; create a product image the prospect wants to merge with.
**Elaboration Likelihood Model (ELM)** (Cashvertising) — two routes to attitude change: central (logic) and peripheral (cues); central-route attitudes are more durable.
**Escalation** (Breakthrough) — redefinition that broadens a product's importance/benefits to give it a mass market.
**Evidence** (Cashvertising) — any factual statement/object/opinion not created by you, used as support; works for important/expensive purchases.
**Fear appeal** (Cashvertising) — fear + a credible, achievable solution; four-step recipe: scare, recommend, make effective, make achievable.
**Feature vs. benefit** (Cashvertising) — a feature is a component; a benefit is what the prospect gets (WIIFM).
**Flesch Reading Ease Score (FRES)** (Cashvertising) — 1-100 readability; higher = easier; aim ~72+, ~11-word sentences, 70-80% one-syllable words.
**Forces of Change** (Breakthrough) — style, mass education — trends that rise and reverse; require timing and intuition.
**Functional Product** (Breakthrough) — the product in action — the series of benefits on which the prospect buys.
**Future pacing** (Vosler) — help the prospect visualize their vastly improved future.
**Gradualization** (Breakthrough) — technique 3: make claims believable before stating them by leading from accepted facts through a succession of more remote facts.
**Heuristics / Length-Implies-Strength** (Cashvertising) — mental shortcuts; long copy with many facts/testimonials is judged more credible by quantity alone.
**Hero's Journey** (Vosler) — the 12-phase story structure (Campbell) adapted to understand the audience's path.
**Identification** (Breakthrough) — technique 2: build a saleable personality into the product by capitalizing on longing for expression/recognition.
**Identification Headline** (Breakthrough) — calls the market together by echoing an emotion/attitude/satisfaction; used for unaware markets.
**Imitation Game** (Vosler) — crafting copy by imitating the structure of trending content.
**Inoculation Theory** (Cashvertising) — reinforce attitudes by presenting a weak attack the prospect defends against, strengthening the position.
**Intensification** (Breakthrough) — technique 1: presenting a series of fresh, new, different fulfillments for the dominant desire.
**Jugular objection** (Vosler) — the single most common objection; go for it first.
**Kintsugi Revision Process** (Vosler) — treat breakage as part of the object's history; edit the second draft.
**Know Like Trust Challenge Convert (KLTCC)** (Vosler) — the sales sequence: know, like, trust, challenge, convert.
**The Language of Logic** (Breakthrough) — words like "therefore," "here's why," "this has been proved" that carry conviction regardless of content.
**Life-Force 8 (LF8)** (Cashvertising) — eight biologically hardwired primary desires (survival, food, freedom from fear, sex, comfort, superiority, care of loved ones, social approval); drive more sales than all other wants combined.
**Market Sophistication** (Breakthrough) — how many similar products the market has been told about before yours.
**Mass Desire** (Breakthrough) — the public spread of a private want; the force that makes advertising work.
**Means-End Chain** (Cashvertising) — people buy for the end benefit, not the product; sell the "benefit of the benefit."
**Mechanism** (Breakthrough) — the way the claim is accomplished; the new device that makes an old claim fresh.
**Mechanization** (Breakthrough) — technique 5: verbally proving the product does what you claim — the "How does it work?" answer.
**Mental movie** (Cashvertising) — the internal visual experience your words install in a prospect's head; the first use of any product happens in the mind.
**Message Sideness** (Cashvertising) — two-sided messages (defend your position AND attack competition) are more persuasive if fair-minded.
**New Mechanism** (Breakthrough) — a new way to make an old promise work; the Stage 3 strategy.
**Permanent Forces** (Breakthrough) — mass instinct and mass technological problems — desires that never fade.
**Physical Product** (Breakthrough) — the tangible object you deliver; only an excuse for charging your price.
**Point of Difference** (Breakthrough) — the new mechanism/quality/freedom that distinguishes your product and gives the claim fresh believability.
**Price Reduction** (Breakthrough) — redefinition that switches the comparison to a more expensive standard to make a price seem less.
**Primary Image** (Breakthrough) — the image a product already has in the prospect's mind; the raw material and bridge for new images.
**Psychological pricing** (Cashvertising) — odd-end prices (77, 95, 99) suggest value; rounded prices suggest prestige.
**PVA (Powerful Visual Adjective)** (Cashvertising) — vivid, sensory adjectives that create mental movies.
**Redefinition** (Breakthrough) — technique 4: removing objections by giving the product a new definition — "this rather than that."
**Reinforcement** (Breakthrough) — making two claims do the work of four by juxtaposing emotional images (1 + 1 can equal 10).
**Restorative Power of the Market** (Breakthrough) — the market renews itself; the desire never fades, only the method of tapping it.
**Simplification** (Breakthrough) — redefinition that makes the hard seem easy.
**Splinter Market** (Breakthrough) — a sub-segment a smaller competitor can attack to avoid competing for the core.
**State of Awareness** (Breakthrough) — how much the market knows about your product and the satisfaction it performs.
**Teach, Transform, Transact** (Vosler) — teaching earns trust; transform understanding, then transact.
**The Three Questions** (Breakthrough) — the analytical core: (1) the mass desire, (2) the state of awareness, (3) the state of sophistication.
**Transfer** (Cashvertising) — borrow credibility by associating with symbols/images/institutions of authority.
**Transtheoretical Model (TTM)** (Cashvertising) — five stages: Precontemplation, Contemplation, Preparation, Action, Maintenance; persuade one stage at a time.
**The Turn** (Breakthrough) — the delicate transition where the prospect's world is joined to the product.
**Understatement** (Breakthrough) — a mood of simplicity, few adjectives, short sentences; escapes the hard-sell stereotype.
**USP** (Cashvertising) — Unique Selling Proposition; what makes you different and preferable.
**V-A-K-O-G** (Cashvertising) — five internal representations: Visual, Auditory, Kinesthetic, Olfactory, Gustatory.
**Verbalization** (Breakthrough) — increasing a headline's impact by how it's stated (the 38 ways).
**Verification** (Breakthrough) — arranging documentation within copy for greatest immediate acceptance and emotional effect.
**Vicarious Desire Fulfillment** (Cashvertising) — people enjoy reading how others satisfied LF8 desires; lets them pre-experience the payoff.
**Vocal Editing Technique** (Vosler) — edit for clarity by reading your copy aloud.
**WIIFM** (Cashvertising) — "What's In It For Me" — the question every prospect is silently asking.
**Zero-risk bias** (Vosler) — people prefer complete elimination of a risk even when alternatives reduce risk more.
